# Ledgerline manual

*Version 2026-11-08 · 152. This manual is updated together with every new version of the app; the version number in **Settings → Backup & export** should match.*

Ledgerline is your personal budget and wealth app: one web page (`index.html`) on GitHub Pages, plus a small price updater that runs on GitHub. Your data never leaves your devices except as an encrypted sync file in your own GitHub account. Sections 1 to 13 are for you; section 14 is for an AI assistant or developer working on the app.

## 1. Getting started

**What lives where.** Your public GitHub repository (`aaron-sn01/ledgerline`) holds the app (`index.html`), this manual (`MANUAL.md`, German `MANUAL.de.md`), the price updater (`fetch_prices.py`, `fetch_compositions.py`, `.github/workflows/update-prices.yml`), the fund lists (`funds.txt`: one ISIN per line, or a coin's symbol such as `ETH`; `tickers.json`: funds with their exact symbol) and the files the updater writes (`prices.json`, `price-history.json`, `compositions.json`; never edit these). Your data (entries, plan, holdings, debts, settings) is stored in the app on each device. The code contains **no personal data**: a new device starts empty and gets your data from sync or a backup. Never write anything personal into the repository.

**Install the app** so it opens full screen with its own icon:

- **iPhone / iPad**: in **Safari**, tap **Share** → **Add to Home Screen** → **Add**.
- **Mac**: in **Safari** (macOS 14 or later) **File → Add to Dock**; in **Chrome** **⋮ → Cast, save and share → Create shortcut…**, tick **Open as window**.
- **Windows**: in **Edge** **⋯ → Apps → Install this site as an app**; Chrome as on the Mac. **Android**: Chrome **⋮ → Add to Home screen**.

The installed app keeps its **own data**, separate from the same page in a browser tab; move data between them with sync or a backup. **Manual → Install the app** shows the steps for the device you're on. A **GitHub account** is only needed for sync and the automations; everything else works on one device without it.

**Friends and family** can use your link (https://aaron-sn01.github.io/ledgerline/). Their data stays on their devices; nobody sees anyone else's. The setup wizard opens the first time (every step can be skipped; **Settings → Backup & export → Run the setup again** reopens it). For sync and automations each person needs **their own** GitHub account and token. They get new versions automatically. Funds they hold need to be in your `funds.txt` for automatic prices (section 7), or they type the price.

**Privacy and security.** Data is stored only on the device, protected by the device lock (add **Settings → App lock** for more). Sync is optional and encrypted with your passphrase (AES-256) before it reaches your GitHub account; without the passphrase the sync copy can't be read or recovered. Imports are read on the device; files are never uploaded. The reading tools are checked against their published fingerprints, and the app may only contact GitHub, CoinGecko, the ECB rates service and the tools' servers. Waiting automation items (an amount and shop, a PayPal email's text, a shared file) sit unencrypted in your private sync file until Ledgerline picks them up, then they're deleted. Clearing browser data or removing the home-screen app deletes that device's data, so keep backups (section 12). Turn on two-factor authentication for your GitHub account.

## 2. Everyday use

- **Overview**: the key figures (left to spend today, this month's spending, savings rate, net worth, cash, debt; tap a card for its page or breakdown), the safety-net badge, **How it's going** (what's on track ✓ and what's worth a look; only shown when there's something to say) and **Ways to save**.
- **Today**: today's budget, **Add an entry**, **Rest of the month** (payments still to come, likely spending and what's likely left), today's entries, and **Can I afford it?**. The arrows next to the date show other days; new entries go on the day you're looking at.
- **Month**: six key figures (tap **Savings rate** for its breakdown), **Monthly budget** (each step of the calculation), **Where the money goes**, **Daily spending**, **By category** with 50/35/15, **Entries** and **Edit this month's plan**.
- **Year**: the year's figures, **Where the money went**, **Month by month** (switch between **Chart** and **Table**; tap a month in the table to open it), **Categories this year** and 50/35/15.
- **Wealth**: net worth, **Safety net**, **Wealth over time**, **Diversification**, **Holdings**, **Investment returns**, **Cash savings**, **Where this could lead**. **Year**, **Debt** and **Settings** are under **More** on a phone.

**Adding an entry**: type a name and amount; Ledgerline suggests a category and learns from every correction. Amounts can be sums (4.20+3.80). An entry paid from savings (a trip) is chosen under **Pay from**. **Undo** appears for a few seconds after deleting or importing. Every box has a **?** that explains it, and texts like "Settings → Recurring plan" are links to that box. **Take the tour** (Settings → Backup & export, or More) walks through the app; **What's new** lists the changes once after each update. **Setup help for your AI** (Manual page) is a text for ChatGPT, Claude or similar that guides you through installing, sync and the Apple Pay automation without asking for your token, passphrase or bank data.

**Monthly review**: opens by itself after a month ends. Correct share counts there (and your cash total, if you don't track accounts one by one); **Done** saves them and marks the month as reviewed. Leftovers and shortfalls count in your savings wherever the money sits, so there's nothing to move.

## 3. Importing

**Ways to import**: **Import → Choose files** (screenshots, PDF statements, bank CSV or Excel files, fund holdings lists, several at once); **Paste screenshot** (take a screenshot, tap its thumbnail, **Done → Copy and Delete**, then paste; on a Mac ⌘V works, also for PDFs); **Share → Send to Ledgerline** from any app, or a **double tap on the back of the iPhone** (section 4). Everything is read on the device; nothing is added until you confirm.

**The review**: check each row, untick what you don't want, then **Add selected**. Each row offers only the types that fit (spent; money in to the budget or to savings; refund; between my accounts; don't add; investments their own); **More…** shows all. Money in can be linked to one of your **planned incomes** and money out to a **fixed cost**: it then counts as that plan line instead of being added twice, and Ledgerline remembers the name for next time. **Add a missed entry** adds a row by hand. Entries from before the month you started tracking are unticked; **Start tracking from …** moves your start back (those months use your current plan).

**Refunds**: money in from a shop you bought from in the last three months or so (a returned hoodie, a partial refund) is matched to that purchase: when you add it, the purchase is reduced by the refund, or removed if it's the full amount, so it counts neither as spending nor as money in. The note on the row says which purchase. To choose the purchase yourself (a health insurance refund for a doctor's bill), set the row's type to **Refund**, then pick the purchase (**Refund of …**) in the same list; **Refund** without a purchase counts it as negative spending in its category instead. A refund you already added: open it on the Month page and pick the purchase under **Refund of** (suggestions first, then every payment; the search finds a name, amount or date); the purchase is reduced (or removed) and the refund entry goes.

**Deselect all** (next to **To add**) unticks every entry; **Select all** (in the entries not added) ticks all of those except duplicates and internal moves.

**Duplicates**: an entry already in Ledgerline, or on two of the files you import, is unticked with a note naming the entry it matches. Repeated purchases (three €3 tickets) stay separate; overlapping screenshots of the same app add each payment once. The same payment is also found under another name: one you typed in within a day, an unusual exact amount within two days, a refund, a card time shifted by an hour or two, and a **PayPal payment that the bank books later as "PayPal …"** (within four days). Tick a row if it was unticked wrongly.

**Accounts in imports**: a balance on a screenshot (Sparkasse "Kontostand" or start screen, Trade Republic **Cash**, Scalable "Guthaben", comdirect, N26 "Current balance") appears under **Account balances**, compared with what Ledgerline expects; tick it to save it. The newest balance per account wins; a date after today counts as today. An account Ledgerline doesn't know opens a window first: **Save** adds it with that balance (or links it to one of your accounts), **Don't track it** leaves it out. Money moved between your own banks (your name on the transfer, in any spelling, set under **Settings → Budget & goal**) gets the type **Between my accounts**: it stays out of your budget and moves the account named in the review.

**What each app gives**

- **Trade Republic**: transaction list (round-ups and saveback counted, "Upcoming" and declined payments skipped; if a declined payment is already in Ledgerline, for example from Apple Pay, the review points to it so you can delete it), one opened investment ("You invested": the exact shares), one opened payment, the **Cash** tab (balance), portfolio and position screens, PDF statements, and **Wealth → Private Markets** (each fund's value; your shares stay, the price is worked out).
- **Sparkasse**: transaction list, one opened payment, the start screen with all accounts, PDF statements. A fixed cost in your plan (rent, phone) is recognised and skipped.
- **N26**: Main Account and Instant Savings lists; round-ups go to savings once a day, moves to and from savings are your own transfers, declined payments are skipped. **comdirect**: transaction list and account overview (the depot is left out). **Scalable Capital**: position (exact shares), Tagesgeld, portfolio and transaction lists. **Wise** (home screen), **Finanzguru** ("Alle Buchungen"; import either Finanzguru or the banks it collects, not both for the same period), **flatex**, **Coinbase**.
- **PayPal**: the Activity list; transfers to and from your bank are skipped, foreign amounts converted at that day's rate.
- **Bank CSV or Excel exports** (N26, Sparkasse/Volksbank, comdirect and most others): the easiest way to add past months. All rows of the file, also those from before you started, also give that account's balances back to the file's first day for **Wealth over time** (export up to today, or at least into the months you track: an account with no payments since, such as an emptied Tagesgeld, then counts from today's balance minus what Ledgerline recorded since; an account you later remove from Ledgerline, because you closed it, keeps counting in the past; Sparkasse: **Umsätze → Filter → Zeitraum**, then **Exportieren → Excel (CSV-CAMT V2)**; a file named "Tagesgeld" goes to your Tagesgeld account). **comdirect** puts several accounts in one file (**Umsätze → Export**, longest time range): each account gets its own payments and its balance ("Neuer Kontostand"), pending rows are left out, a card payment gets the day you paid, and money moved between your two accounts on the same day isn't spending. Its depot section has no purchases; for returns, export **Depotumsätze** separately.
- **Transaction histories of brokers**, for **Investment returns** (section 7): Trade Republic (its transaction export, CSV), flatex (**Umsätze → Depotumsätze**, time range from your first purchase, **Excel** or CSV), Coinbase (**Statements → Transaction history**, CSV), Scalable Capital (**Transactions → Export**, CSV; a depot moved within Scalable nets out, cancelled and rejected orders are left out). The Scalable export covers the broker account only: for the **Scalable Tagesgeld**, import its monthly statements (Kontoauszüge, PDF, several at once), which give its balance on every day. They're not reviewed row by row: each holding's purchases, sales, dividends and rewards are kept for the returns, and Trade Republic's cash account for Wealth over time. A newer file adds only what's new.
- **Fund holdings lists** (iShares CSV or Excel): update a fund's sectors and countries. If the file doesn't name its fund (iShares' newer downloads), Ledgerline asks **which fund it is for**, with the likely one first (from the file name, e.g. SWDA → Core MSCI World, or from what the list holds).
- **Any other bank**: every line with a date and an amount is offered, marked "layout not known yet: please check each line". A sample screenshot (personal details blacked out) lets the maintainer add a proper reader.

Dark-mode screenshots work too; money in is also recognised by its green colour. Anything unclear is marked for you to check.

## 4. Automations (iPhone)

All automations need sync on that iPhone (section 11). The steps by hand (iOS 17 and later), the two values to paste (with **Copy** buttons) and a ready prompt for the AI in Shortcuts (iOS 27; it uses placeholders, so your token never goes into a prompt) are in **Settings → Automations**. Keep that page open while you work in the **Shortcuts** app.

- **Apple Pay at the card terminal**: a Shortcuts automation (**Transaction**, "When I tap my card") sends amount and shop after each payment with iPhone or Watch at a terminal; online and in-app Apple Pay isn't included. Choose **Ask me to confirm** or **Add straight away**.
- **PayPal by email**: a Mail automation (sender `service@paypal.de` and `service@intl.paypal.com`; a **Text** action builds the line from the email's subject, sender and content, **Get Contents of URL** sends it) passes each PayPal email to Ledgerline, which reads the shop or person, the amount and currency, the direction, and the bank that paid ("Gezahlt mit: Sparkasse …", so the entry goes to that account). PayPal's emails must arrive in the **Mail app on the iPhone** by themselves: providers that only offer **Fetch** (web.de, GMX) need **Fetch New Data → Every 15 Minutes**. The formal form ("Sie haben 6,99 € EUR an Netflix.com gezahlt") is read too. Like Apple Pay, PayPal has its own choice: **Ask me to confirm** (each payment waits in the review) or **Add straight away**. Emails that aren't payments are listed under **Last emails from PayPal** with their subject and removed.
- **Share to Ledgerline**: a shortcut in the Share menu sends screenshots and PDFs from any app into the import; several files in one share work. The import opens by itself the next time you open Ledgerline (or tap **Sync now**).
- **Automatic backup on your Mac**: a Shortcuts automation on the Mac saves your encrypted sync file into a folder once a month (section 12).
- **Back Tap** (iPhone 8 and later, iOS 14 or later): a second, two-action shortcut (**Take Screenshot**, then **Run Shortcut** "Send to Ledgerline" with the screenshot as input), set under the iPhone's **Settings → Accessibility → Touch → Back Tap → Double Tap**. In your banking app, double-tap the back of the iPhone and the screen goes straight to the import.

**Is it working?** Run an automation by hand once (▶ in Shortcuts) and allow the connection to api.github.com: the next time you open Ledgerline it says "Apple Pay automation works: the test arrived", or for PayPal "reached Ledgerline, without an email's content" (a run by hand has no email). A real PayPal email must then show up as a payment under **Last emails from PayPal**; if it only ever arrives empty, the variables in the **Text** action are missing. **Settings → Automations** shows **Last arrived** for Apple Pay, PayPal email and Share (on any of your devices) and the Sync ID your automations must send to. If one stops: check its **token** (a new token must also go into every shortcut) and the **Sync ID** in its web address (a new sync file has a new Sync ID); for Apple Pay also **Settings → Apps → Wallet → Mobile Data** on the iPhone and **Run Immediately**; for PayPal that the email account is in the iPhone's Mail app. Adding **Quick Look** after **Get Contents of URL** shows GitHub's answer: "Bad credentials" means the token, "Not Found" the address.

## 5. Plan, budget and goal

**Recurring plan** (**Settings → Recurring plan**): income, fixed costs, annual costs and investment plans, each with its real day of the month. A change applies at once to this month and every later month, item by item; what you changed for one month by hand (**Month → Edit this month's plan**) stays, and past months never change.

- **Changes from a later month** (a new job, a move): in **Settings → Recurring plan** pick the month and tap **Plan a change from this month**, then edit that plan; or open that month, change **Edit this month's plan** and tap **Use this plan from … on**. Until then the plan above applies; from that month on, the changed one. Later changes to the plan above also reach it, unless you changed that line. When the month comes, the change becomes your recurring plan.
- **Use as my recurring plan** (in **Edit this month's plan**) makes a month's plan your recurring plan. If the recurring plan is ever empty while this month has a plan, Settings offers to take it. **Replace whole months** (for a past month, or to undo single-month changes) copies the recurring plan into months from a given one.
- **Paid in advance** (a stipend paid on the 29th for the next month): enter the real day and tick it. It counts for the month it's meant for and is kept aside until then; imports recognise it as next month's income.
- **Accounts**: with two or more accounts, choose the **main account** at the top and, under each line, the account it's **paid into / from** (empty = main account).
- **Suggestions from imports**: when a planned item arrives on a clearly different day or amount three months in a row, or a payment repeats regularly without being in the plan, the Overview offers to update or add it (**Change** / **Keep**).

**Monthly budget**: income and other money in, minus fixed costs (annual costs spread over the year), investment plans, round-ups, debt repayments and the money **set aside for your goal**. The daily budget follows your usual spending by weekday. In the last three days of a month, **Left to spend today** shows your usual daily amount, and the rest is named as going to savings. Projections ("Day-to-day spending still to come", likely to savings, the projected savings rate) use your own spending of the last months; big one-offs (at least €100 and five times your usual purchase) aren't spread over the remaining days.

**Savings-rate goal** (**Settings → Budget & goal**, also changing from a given month): its cash part is set aside before your spending budget, after what investment plans, round-ups and repayments already cover. **Money in to savings** (interest, a windfall you keep) never changes the budget. If you want to spend part of such a payment, book it as **Money in, budget**. The **savings rate** is plans, round-ups, debt repaid from income and the month's leftover, minus spending from savings, as a share of your regular income; a second line shows it including money in to savings, and the window (tap the card) also shows the budget alone.

**Categories** (**Settings → Categories**; tap one to edit) are by type of spending and serve both entries and fixed costs ("fixed" belongs to an item, not a category). Each has a colour, an optional monthly limit, whether it's **paid from savings** (travel, gifts) and whether it's a **need** or a **want**.

**50/35/15** (Month and Year): spending sorted into needs (target 50% of income) and wants; **Saved** is the same number as the savings rate (so refunds paid into savings count), with your savings-rate goal as its target (so a 15% goal gives 50/35/15). The current month shows **so far** and **projected**, plus housing as a share of income.

## 6. Cash and accounts

**Cash** is all your accounts today minus what this month still needs (what's left to spend, plus payments still to come, minus income still to arrive), so overspending lowers it at once. Each account follows its money after you save its balance: imported entries move the account of their bank, typed-in entries the main account, planned items the account chosen for them, transfers between your own accounts both sides (once). Saveback doesn't, because it's the broker's money.

- **Correct an account**: import a screenshot of its balance, or **Wealth → Cash savings → Update balance** (add or remove accounts there too). An entry's account can be changed in the entry.
- Without separate accounts (one total only), the total you entered is kept apart from this month's budget, with leftovers and shortfalls joining at month end.
- **Safety net**: your cash divided by one month of needs (fixed costs, investment plans, typical day-to-day spending without one-offs).
- **Net worth change**: tap the Net worth card for the change since the end of last month: cash, money put into investments, market movement and debt.

## 7. Investments and prices

**Prices**: ETF prices update every 2 hours on weekdays (09:23 to 23:23 German time). The updater also keeps every daily price back to 2010 in `price-history.json` (for funds, and for coins listed in `funds.txt` by their symbol, e.g. `ETH Ethereum`); the app loads it only for Investment returns and Wealth over time. **Refresh prices** on Wealth fetches the newest price file; **Actions → Update prices → Run workflow** on GitHub fetches prices now (and fund compositions). Crypto prices come live from CoinGecko. A price showing "—": **Manual → Price updater** lists funds the last run failed for.

**Adding an ETF**: **Wealth → Add holding** (name, ISIN, broker, shares, asset class). Then add its ISIN as a new line to `funds.txt` on GitHub (the window links to it; pencil icon → new line → **Commit changes**), or tap **add to price list** on the Wealth page for the line to paste. If the updater finds no symbol, add the fund to `tickers.json` with its exact Yahoo symbol (Xetra usually ends in `.DE`).

- **Crypto**: **Add holding → Crypto** and type the coin (`BTC`, `Bitcoin`, `Solana` … or its CoinGecko id) and the units.
- **Without automatic price** (private equity, a pension): **Other investment** or **Private equity** with a price you enter; a statement or the Trade Republic Private Markets screenshot updates it.
- **Asset class**: **Asset class → + New asset class…** in the holding window.
- **Savings plans**: in the recurring plan, linked to the holding. Shares are estimated as amount ÷ that day's price until an import shows the shares actually bought (Trade Republic "You invested", Scalable lists, statements). Tap a holding for its **Recent purchases** (≈ = estimated).

**Investment returns** (Wealth): import your brokers' transaction histories (section 3). The box shows the **gain** in euros since your first purchase (value today + what came back from sales and dividends − what you paid in, with fees), the return **per year** (money-weighted: each payment counts from its own date) and **in total**, a table **by year** (value at the start, money in, gain, return, value at the end; the current year "so far") and one **by holding** (tap a row to show only that one). **Show** at the top chooses all investments, ETFs and funds, crypto, private equity or one holding. **By year** and **By holding** fold away separately (remembered on this device). **Hide** next to a holding takes it out of the list (the totals still include it); **Show again** under the table brings it back. The notes, the explanation and the how-to are in the small grey **Notes and explanation** fold. Saveback, staking and rewards cost you nothing and count as gain; a coin swapped for another is a sale of one and a purchase of the other (inside crypto it's no money in or out). Shares transferred from another depot (flatex **WP-Einbuchung**) have no purchase date: Ledgerline matches each lot's date from its purchase price and the fund's daily prices (savings plans on fixed days, in the order the broker lists the lots); the note under the box says how close the match is. If a history adds up to a different number of shares than your holding, **Use the history's number** corrects the holding. Coins and private equity without daily prices are valued at the last known price (private equity: the value you enter on a holding with the same ISIN). Sold fractions before a depot transfer and anything not in the file can't be counted. **After a history ends** the box continues by itself with what Ledgerline records: purchases, round-ups and saveback from screenshots (exact shares), savings-plan executions (estimated until a screenshot or statement shows the shares), sales, and Coinbase rewards from a screenshot of its Transactions list (coins and euros). A screenshot with your share count (flatex depot, Coinbase coin page) corrects the count: more shares than expected count as a purchase at that day's price (coins: staking), fewer as a sale. Better sources win and nothing is counted twice: a CSV or statement over a detail screenshot, that over a list screenshot or the plan, that over a guess from a share count. So a CSV is only needed once; a flatex Wertpapierabrechnung (PDF) gives each purchase exactly, and the depot screen's Einstandswert is checked against the money in. **Remove** (under **Notes and explanation**) deletes one broker's history. In **Wealth over time**, after you started tracking, a purchase from a history shows on its own date.

**Sectors and countries** (Wealth → Diversification; on big screens all four donuts side by side, on phones and narrow windows the chips switch between **Sectors**, **Countries**, **Asset types** and **Developed vs emerging**): Xtrackers funds update themselves monthly. iShares blocks automatic downloads: on the iShares website open the fund → **Holdings** → download the file and import it (section 3); once or twice a year is enough. Common funds have approximate factsheet values built in. **Explanation** under the box lists each fund's source and date.

## 8. Charts, boxes and display

- **Where the money goes** (Month) and **Where the money went** (Year): every euro from where it came from to where it went; both sides add up. Money from cash savings runs as one stream to **Paid from savings**; money in to savings (interest) has its own stream to **Kept in savings**. Payments from savings are first paid from what came into savings in the same period, then from a finished month's leftover, then from the goal set-aside; only the rest shows as **From cash savings** or **Shortfall from savings**. Each category appears once on the right. While a month is open its unspent budget shows as **Not spent yet**. On a phone the diagram keeps its full size and scrolls sideways.
- **Charts are drawn at the width they're shown at**, so their labels keep their size on every screen; only the money-flow diagram scrolls sideways on a phone. Tap (or hover) a chart for details; lists that already show the numbers have no pop-ups.
- **Wealth over time** (1M to All, 3M at first): the summary adds up: saved (in cash and into investments), market movement and debt. The dashed part before you started tracking is an estimate from today's holdings, or, with imported histories, built from them: investments from your brokers' histories, cash only from accounts with an imported history (the note under the chart names them), so it can be lower there. **All** goes back to the earliest of these.
- **Where this could lead**: your investments projected to an age, with the middle outcome, the range of 80% of outcomes and what you paid in.
- **Boxes**: tap a title to close or open a box; it stays that way on this device. **Rearrange boxes** (bottom of each page) moves boxes with ↑ ↓ (or by dragging ⠿ with a mouse) and can **Hide** boxes you don't need (**Show** brings them back). Settings and Manual boxes, Entries and Where this could lead start closed. Longer explanations are folded under **Explanation**.
- **Settings**: language buttons at the top, a search box (titles first, then content) and the chips **Plan**, **Devices**, **Advanced**.
- **Light or dark**: the round button at the top right (follows your device until you tap it). **Language**: English or Deutsch at the top of Settings; German also uses German dates and number format. **PDF summary**: More → PDF summary (or a page's PDF button), over as many A4 pages as needed.

## 9. Currencies

Everything shows in your **home currency** (**Settings → Budget & goal → Currency**). With **I use more than one currency** ticked, entries, plan lines, accounts, holdings and debts can each have their own currency: amounts stay as typed (5 GBP) and are converted at that day's ECB rate (frankfurter.app, plus the Gulf currencies pegged to the dollar), with the converted amount shown next to them. Wealth then also lists each currency on its own. With one currency, nothing changes.

## 10. Debt

**Debt → Add a debt**: the amount left, the interest rate (0 for interest-free) and optionally a target date and the day it **started** (Wealth over time shows no debt before that day). **Repay with a fixed monthly instalment** makes it a fixed cost each month that comes off the balance after interest. One-off repayments: **Record a repayment**, from savings or from the month's budget.

## 11. Sync between devices

Sync keeps your devices in step through an encrypted file in **your own free GitHub account**. Set it up once, in about 10 minutes:

1. **GitHub account**: github.com/signup (skip if you have one).
2. **Token**: github.com/settings/tokens/new → **Tokens (classic)**; Note "Ledgerline"; **Expiration: No expiration** (otherwise sync and every automation stop when it expires); tick **only gist**; **Generate token**; copy it (starts with `ghp_`) into your password manager.
3. **First device**: **Settings → Sync between devices**: the token, a passphrase (at least 8 characters, kept in your password manager; it can't be recovered), Sync ID empty, **Create sync file**. The **Sync ID** then shows there with **Copy**.
4. **Every other device**: the same token, that Sync ID and the same passphrase, then **Connect**.

All devices must show the same Sync ID. **Show token** on a connected device reveals the token to paste on the next one; the passphrase is never stored. A **new sync file always has a new Sync ID**: change it on your other devices and in every automation. Settings sync part by part: changing one part on one device (the language, your name) doesn't overwrite another part changed on another device (your recurring plan). Keep all devices on the same version (open the app and tap **Reload** when it offers a new one).

## 12. App lock, updates and backups

**App lock** (**Settings → App lock**): Face ID or Touch ID with a PIN as fallback, per device; it locks after 2 minutes in the background.

**Updating the app**: in the repository **Add file → Upload files**, drop in `index.html` (exactly that name) and the two manuals, **Commit changes**. When **Actions → pages build and deployment** shows a green tick (1 to 2 minutes), the app offers **New version available → Reload** (when it opens, when you return to it, or within 30 minutes). Never remove the app from the Dock or home screen to update it: that deletes its data. Your data carries over and is upgraded automatically.

**Backups**: **Settings → Backup & export → Download backup** after each monthly review; **Restore from backup** brings it back (newer edits win). With sync on, **Automatic backup on your Mac** (**Settings → Automations**) saves your encrypted sync file monthly through a Shortcuts automation; GitHub also keeps every earlier version of the sync file.

**Demo data** (**Settings → Backup & export → Preview with demo data**) adds sample entries without touching yours; **Remove demo data** deletes exactly those. **Erase all data in this browser** affects only the browser or app you press it in.

## 13. Troubleshooting

- **Prices look old**: section 7; a red run under **Actions → Update prices** shows the reason, and the next run usually fixes it.
- **App didn't update**: the file must be named exactly `index.html`; wait for the green tick, then reload. Deployments stuck on "Queued": check githubstatus.com.
- **Sync problem** (the pill at the top): **Settings → Sync between devices** shows the reason.
